How the page works

Method notes for your project report

1 · Build candidate schedules

Each candidate assigns every exam paper to one of two daily sessions: 08:00–09:30 or 12:00–13:30. The selected date range supplies the available weekdays.

2 · Check hard constraints

The generator checks shared-student exam clashes and whether the per-session hall demand exceeds 40 rooms. Each listed room holds 60 students; large papers are split across multiple halls.

3 · Reduce undesirable patterns

Among feasible schedules, it reduces each student's consecutive-session exam pairs, then prefers more even total enrollment across sessions.

4 · Compare search methods

GA uses selection, crossover, and mutation. ES mutates candidates and selects survivors. EP mutates parent candidates and uses pairwise competition. They share a score and search budget.

Fitness formula

Student clashes × 1,000,000 + hall overflow × 1,000,000 + back-to-back pairs × 100 + load imbalance × 1. Best is ranked feasibility first, then back-to-back pairs, then load balance.

Data and limits

Course lists, USNs, enrollments, and rooms are synthetic draft data. Dates omit weekends. Use official course registrations, room availability, holidays, and exam duration rules before treating a schedule as real.
